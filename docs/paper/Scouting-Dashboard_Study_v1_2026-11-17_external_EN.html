The value in one sentence. The dashboard shows a club which players below the professional leagues are worth a close look, before others discover them and before they become expensive.

Why this makes money. (1) Buy cheaper: players from the third to fifth tier and from academies outside the big names cost little or nothing. A club that spots them early pays less and gains on resale. (2) More points: a stronger line-up wins measurably more often, 58% instead of 32% of matches. Points decide promotion, survival and prize money. (3) Scouting time where it pays: instead of screening 15,089 players, the scout starts from a list with three times the hit rate. (4) No data costs: the basis is public match reports. Event data does not exist for these leagues anyway.

Why it can be trusted. Both claims were tested without looking into the future: ratings were frozen at a cut-off date and compared with what happened afterwards, on 6,021 later matches and on 1,122 youth players in two time windows. A player's value is the difference he makes to his team's goal difference, adjusted for teammates and opponents. The method does not replace the scout or the video. It tells you where both are worth it.

2 Starting point and objective

Anyone assessing players from the Regionalliga (third tier), a Landesliga (fourth tier) or an academy faces a gap. The professional leagues have event data on every pass and betting odds on every match. Below them, neither exists. Scouting there relies on live observation, on goals and on recommendations. That is expensive, patchy and favours strikers, because only goals are counted.

The objective of the dashboard is a reliable shortlist: from thousands of players, find those worth a close look. For that, the rating must do three things. It must be available for every league, it must capture defenders and midfielders as well as goal scorers, and it must demonstrably say something about the future.

4 How results turn into player values

The method is called plus-minus and comes from basketball and ice hockey. Hvattum (2020) and Hvattum, Kriegl and Čulík (2023) developed it for football. The idea is simple: you do not observe what a player does on the ball, but what happens to the score while he is on the pitch.

Step 1: Build segments. Every match is split at every substitution. 12,883 matches yield several tens of thousands of segments with a fixed line-up.

Step 2: Rate everyone at once. One calculation looks for the values of all players simultaneously that best explain the goals scored. Because all 22 players of a segment are in the model together, nobody is rewarded for playing in a strong team and nobody is punished for playing in a weak one. Home advantage and numerical advantage are separate terms.

Step 3: Caution with little data. A player with few minutes stays close to average. Only many minutes allow a value that moves clearly away from it. This prevents flukes after three good matches.

Step 4: Account for age. Players improve until about 26. The peak value projects today's value to that age, so that a 17-year-old can be compared with a 27-year-old. The talent rank then orders players within their birth year.

Key takeaways

  • What is rated is the impact on the result, not the action on the ball.
  • Teammates, opponents, home advantage and numerical advantage are removed.
  • Little playing time leads to a cautious value close to average.

5 From player values to win probability

In professional football, betting odds are the best available estimate of how likely a team is to win. They pool the knowledge of many market participants. Odds give the win probability, and the win probability gives the expected points. To assess a team, one therefore asks: is it above or below what the market expected?

Below the professional leagues this market does not exist. The dashboard arrives at the same quantity the other way round. It starts with the players and builds the team from them.

The chain in four links.

  1. Player value. Every player has a value in goals per 90 minutes.
  2. Line-up strength. The sum of the eleven values is the strength of the line-up. The model is built so that this sum is directly an expected goal difference.
  3. Win probability. The difference between two line-ups gives the probabilities of win, draw and loss.
  4. Points. Three points times the win probability plus one point times the draw probability gives the expected points.

What follows. A team raises its win probability by fielding players with higher values. The reverse also holds: teams that win more than expected have players on the pitch who contribute more than they were given credit for. The model assigns this surplus to the players who were on the pitch when it arose. A player in whose appearances the team's goal difference is regularly better than expected with those teammates and against those opponents receives a high value. These are exactly the players a scout is looking for.

Team strength and player value are two views. A pure team strength from results, as measured by an Elo rating, answers “How strong is this team?”. It cannot say who is responsible. The player value answers exactly that. Section 6 tests whether both views lead to the same result.

Key takeaways

  • Betting odds give the win probability top-down; player values give it bottom-up.
  • The sum of the eleven player values is an expected goal difference and therefore a win probability.
  • Only the route via the players shows who makes up a team's strength.

6 Evidence 1: Line-up strength predicts results

Test design. Player values were computed only from matches up to June 2025, that is from two seasons. Then they were frozen. The test used 6,021 later matches from the seasons 2025/26 and 2026/27 that the model had never seen. For each match only the starting eleven was used, as known before kick-off. On average 69% of the starters already had a value; all others counted as average players.

The values are right in size as well. The model claims: one goal of difference in the line-up totals means one goal of difference in the result. Measured: 1.18 goals (uncertainty ± 0.12). The player values can therefore be read in real goals and are cautious rather than exaggerated.

Comparison with pure team strength. The benchmark is a team strength that learns from results only (Elo). Compared is the forecast error for win, draw and loss on the matches of season 2026/27. The conversion into probabilities was calibrated on season 2025/26.

How to read this. Player values are not a betting model. For forecasting the next match, a continuously updated team strength is superior, as one would expect. The decisive point is a different one: values that are more than a year old and cover only two thirds of the starters explain results as well as the team strength of the same date. A large part of a team's strength therefore sits in the individual values of its players, and only these can be transferred to another club.

Key takeaways

  • Clearly stronger line-up: 58% home wins. Clearly weaker: 32%.
  • One goal of difference in player values corresponds to a measured 1.18 goals in the result.
  • At the same state of knowledge, player values are as accurate as team strength and additionally show who carries it.
  • +0.10 goals per match in the line-up is about 1.7 points per season.

What the numbers say. The top 2% break through about three times as often as the average, the top 10% almost twice as often, the bottom half clearly less often. Ranking by minutes played alone finds hardly more than the average at the top. The added value therefore comes from the rating, not from playing time alone.

The check by name is shown in figure 1: of the ten top-rated players from window 1, 9 broke through; among the top 30 it is 17, among the top 50 24.

Putting the often-quoted 80% in context. The literature states that the top 20% by this method contain about 80% of the later most valuable players. In our data the top 20% contain 30% (window 1) and 37% (window 2) of the later risers. There are two reasons for the difference: our sample contains only players who already play a lot, and the observation period of two years is short. For practice the hit rate is the more important number: it says how often a close look pays off.

Key takeaways

  • Top 2%: 9 of 11 with a breakthrough (window 1), 8 of 12 (window 2). Average: 26% and 21%.
  • Of the ten top-rated players, 7 play in a professional league.
  • Minutes played alone do not achieve this. The added value comes from the rating.

8 Why this works without event data

Event data describes what a player does on the ball. It is expensive to collect and therefore available only for the upper leagues. The plus-minus method does not need it, because it starts from the result and not from the action.

Table 8. Event data and results data compared.

The real advantage. The two kinds of data complement each other. The method does not replace the scout or the video. It says for whom both are worth it. In a market of more than 15,000 players that is the bottleneck: nobody can watch them all. Section 7 shows that the shortlist roughly triples the hit rate at the top.

Key takeaways

  • Results data exists for every league, event data only for the upper ones.
  • The method also captures impact that no event records, such as defensive work.
  • It does not replace scout and video, but says where both pay off.

9 Limitations

The results are robust enough for a shortlist. On their own they are not sufficient for a transfer decision. The main limitations:

  1. Short period. The data starts in 2023/24. The longest possible look back covers two years. Whether a player stays in professional football cannot yet be measured.
  2. Small numbers at the top. The top 2% are eleven to twelve players per window. The figures show the uncertainty range. The direction is the same in both windows, the exact level is not.
  3. Breakthrough is only partly visible. Professional leagues and foreign leagues are not part of the match data. Professional status comes from the association's public player profiles and may be out of date. Players who moved abroad count as no breakthrough. The true rates are more likely higher. A comparison with a database on professional football is prepared.
  4. Academy effect. Players from large academies have better chances regardless of their rating. Part of the hit rate is due to this. The comparison within the same academy is still outstanding.
  5. Second teams. A regular place in the second team of a professional club in the Regionalliga counts as a breakthrough here. It can also be seen as a preliminary stage.
  6. Little playing time. Below about 900 minutes a value is preliminary. The dashboard shows this as a traffic light.
  7. No substitute for team strength. For forecasting the next match, a continuously updated team strength is better. Player values answer a different question.

Key takeaways

  • Two years of look back, small groups at the top: direction established, level with uncertainty.
  • Moves abroad and into professional leagues are recorded incompletely; the true rates are more likely higher.
